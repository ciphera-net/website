## What Does a Privacy-First Platform Actually Need From a CDN?

CDNs deliver DNS responses 60% faster on desktop and 19% faster on mobile compared to origin servers, according to the [HTTP Archive Web Almanac](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/cdn) (2025). Speed matters — a 1-second delay in page load time results in a 7% reduction in conversions ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/page-load-time-conversion-rates), 2025). But when your entire product is built around the promise that nobody — not even you — can read your users' files, speed isn't enough.

Here's what our CDN checklist looked like:

- **EU jurisdiction** — headquartered in an EU member state, not just hosting EU data centers
- **No PII logging** — anonymized access logs by default, not as an opt-in
- **Data routing control** — ability to restrict delivery to EU-only edge nodes
- **E2EE compatibility** — works with opaque encrypted blobs, no need to inspect or transform content
- **Transparent pricing** — pay-as-you-go without enterprise sales calls or hidden fees
- **Security certifications** — ISO 27001, SOC 2, or equivalent

Most major CDNs failed at least two of these. Cloudflare is US-headquartered and routes through US infrastructure by default. AWS CloudFront is a subsidiary of the world's largest surveillance-adjacent cloud provider. Fastly is publicly traded in the US with data processing governed by US law. Are they fast? Absolutely. But for a privacy platform, jurisdiction and data handling aren't negotiable.

<BlogBlockquote>

**Our take:** GDPR compliance isn't a feature you bolt onto a US-headquartered service. It's a property of where a company is incorporated, where its data flows, and which courts have jurisdiction over it. That distinction matters more than any checkbox on a compliance page.

</BlogBlockquote>

## How BunnyCDN Fits Into Ciphera's Architecture

The [global CDN market](https://www.precedenceresearch.com/content-delivery-network-market) is valued at $32.70 billion in 2025 and projected to hit $38.75 billion by 2026 (Precedence Research). But most CDN architectures assume they'll inspect, transform, or cache readable content. Ciphera's model is different. Every file uploaded through our platform is encrypted with AES-256-GCM in the user's browser before it ever hits our servers. The CDN never sees plaintext. It caches and delivers opaque encrypted blobs.

<BlogBlockquote>

**How it works:** The browser encrypts the file → uploads to our backend → backend stores the blob in S3-compatible storage → BunnyCDN sits in front as a pull zone → recipients download the encrypted blob from the nearest edge node → their browser decrypts it locally. BunnyCDN never needs to read the file. It just moves encrypted bytes closer to the user.

</BlogBlockquote>

This architecture means BunnyCDN's caching works perfectly — encrypted blobs are static, immutable files with unique URLs. Cache hit ratios stay high. Edge delivery stays fast. And because the CDN can't read the content, there's zero risk of accidental data exposure at the edge. Even if an attacker compromised a BunnyCDN edge node, they'd only find encrypted noise.

We also use BunnyCDN's Routing Filters for files that require EU-only delivery. A single toggle ensures those encrypted blobs are served exclusively from European PoPs. No workaround, no Terraform scripting, no manual edge configuration. It just works.
